Program Manager · Education & Development

I'm a Program Manager at Pratham Education Foundation, working on a state-level initiative to improve learning outcomes in government tribal residential schools across Maharashtra. The program covers 500+ schools and involves everyone from Class 1 students learning to read their first word to government officials deciding how assessments get reported.

Most of what I do is unglamorous: reviewing data, visiting schools, troubleshooting implementation failures, and having the same conversations with teachers and superintendents until something actually changes. I find that more interesting than the version of this work that looks good in slide decks.

Before this I worked in STEM education and community outreach. Born in Bihar, educated in Haryana, and based in Delhi — I have a postgraduate degree in Hindi literature. This academic background turns out to be surprisingly useful in the field.

Pratham Education Foundation

2024 – Present

Program Manager, Maharashtra

Majhi Shala, Majha Swabhimaan is a 3-year initiative to improve foundational learning outcomes in 500+ government tribal residential schools across four divisions of Maharashtra. I run the program, actively overseeing assessment design, teacher training, field team coordination, government liaison, and a live data dashboard for 455+ schools simultaneously.

The hardest part isn't the scale. It's ensuring that what gets implemented on the ground actually matches what was designed. A training workshop is easy to run. The real challenge lies in getting 500 schools to change their teaching practices and systematically verifying those improvements.

What 8,575 Assessments Taught Me About Learning Gaps in Tribal Schools

March 2026

When our team completed midline assessments for over 8,500 students across ashram schools in Maharashtra, the data was both sobering and hopeful. Reading proficiency at the Story level jumped from 14% to 35%, marking a significant learning gain. However, the journey to achieve this revealed something far more important than any metric. The gap between what children are taught and what they actually understand is much wider than most program reports admit. Here's what I observed on the ground, and why honest assessment design is more important than building impressive dashboards.

Why a Wooden Abacus Still Beats a Spreadsheet in the Classroom

February 2026

I've seen a lot of education technology pitches for tablets, learning apps, and AI tutors. Yet some of my most memorable classroom moments from field visits to ashram schools in Amravati and Nashik involved a simple abacus, a set of 3D alphabet letters, and children who couldn't sit still because they were fully engaged in learning. Teaching Learning Materials (TLM) rarely get enough credit in policy conversations. This post explores what hands-on, activity-based learning looks like in practice, along with the specific teacher support required to sustain it.

The Catch-Up Problem: Reaching Children Who Fell Behind Before We Arrived

January 2026

A Class 6 student who cannot read a paragraph hasn't failed. Instead, the system successfully passed them along without ensuring foundational skills were built between Class 1 and now. The Catch-Up Course we've been rolling out across 500+ tribal residential schools in Maharashtra operates on a simple but radical premise: meet children exactly where they are rather than where the syllabus assumes they should be. Scaling this initiative across four divisions has taught me exactly what remedial education demands of teachers, field teams, and the institutions funding these programs.
